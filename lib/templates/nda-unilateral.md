# UNILATERAL (ONE-WAY) NON-DISCLOSURE AGREEMENT

**This Unilateral Non-Disclosure Agreement ("Agreement") is entered into as of {{effective_date}} between:**

- **{{company_name}}**, located at {{company_address}} (the "Disclosing Party"); and

("Unilateral" or "one-way" means only one side is sharing confidential information — the Disclosing Party discloses, and only the Receiving Party takes on confidentiality obligations. If both sides will be sharing sensitive information with each other, use a **Mutual NDA** instead.)

---

## 1. Purpose

> **What this section does:** States exactly *why* the Disclosing Party is sharing confidential information — for example, evaluating a contractor before hiring them, or briefing an advisor so they can give informed guidance. A specific purpose clause limits how the Receiving Party can use what they learn.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Using this one-way template when the relationship is actually two-directional. If the person you're talking to will *also* be sharing their own confidential information with you — their proprietary process, their client list, their own trade secrets — you need a **Mutual NDA**, not this one. This template only protects information flowing from {{company_name}} outward; it creates no obligations at all around anything the Receiving Party shares with you.
>
> *How to fill this in: One or two sentences naming the specific engagement. Examples: "evaluating {{receiving_party_name}} as a potential contractor for [project]" / "briefing an advisor on the Company's product and business plans in connection with an advisory relationship" / "a scoping conversation before a potential engagement."*

The Disclosing Party wishes to share information with the Receiving Party in connection with: {{purpose}} (the "Purpose").

## 2. What Counts as Confidential Information

> **What this section does:** Defines what the Receiving Party must keep secret. Because this is one-way, only the Disclosing Party's information is covered — there is no parallel obligation on the Disclosing Party, because the Disclosing Party isn't receiving anything confidential under this Agreement.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Assuming information is protected only if marked "CONFIDENTIAL." Courts generally apply a "reasonable person" standard — if a reasonable person would obviously understand information is sensitive (unreleased product plans, source code, financial projections, customer data), it's protected even without a label. Still, labeling sensitive materials "Confidential" when you share them removes any ambiguity later.

"Confidential Information" means any non-public information disclosed by the Disclosing Party to the Receiving Party, in any form — written, electronic, oral, or visual — that is:

- **Marked** as confidential, proprietary, or similar at the time of disclosure; **or**
- **Identified** as confidential by the Disclosing Party at or promptly after disclosure; **or**
- **Of a nature** that a reasonable person would understand to be confidential given the context of the disclosure.

Confidential Information includes, without limitation: business plans, financial information, customer and user data, product designs, source code, technical specifications, marketing strategies, and any other non-public information about the Disclosing Party's products, services, or operations.

## 3. What Is NOT Confidential

> **What this section does:** Carves out four standard categories that are never protected, even if they would otherwise fit the definition in Section 2. These carve-outs exist because it would be unreasonable to require the Receiving Party to keep secret information it already knew, that became public through no fault of its own, or that it developed independently.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Trying to retroactively claim information is confidential after it has already become public. Once information is legitimately public, an NDA cannot pull it back into confidentiality.

Information is not protected under this Agreement if it:

- **(a)** Is or becomes publicly available through no act or omission of the Receiving Party;
- **(b)** Was already known to the Receiving Party before the date of disclosure, as shown by written records predating the disclosure;
- **(c)** Is independently developed by the Receiving Party without use of or reference to the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information; or
- **(d)** Is rightfully received from a third party who had the right to disclose it without restriction.

If the Receiving Party is required by law or court order to disclose Confidential Information, it will (to the extent permitted) promptly notify the Disclosing Party and cooperate to seek a protective order before disclosing.

## 4. Obligations of the Receiving Party

> **What this section does:** States exactly what the Receiving Party must do — and not do — with the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information. Because this Agreement is one-way, these obligations run in only one direction: onto the Receiving Party. The Disclosing Party has no confidentiality obligations under this Agreement.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** A contractor or advisor forwarding materials to a subcontractor, co-founder, or friend "just to get a second opinion," without confirming that person is also bound by confidentiality. The Receiving Party may only share with people who have a genuine need to know and are themselves under an equivalent confidentiality obligation.

The Receiving Party agrees to:

- **(a) Purpose limitation:** Use the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information solely for the Purpose described in Section 1 and for no other purpose.
- **(b) Disclosure restriction:** Not disclose the Confidential Information to any third party without the Disclosing Party's prior written consent, except to the Receiving Party's own employees, contractors, or advisors who: (i) have a genuine need to know the information to carry out the Purpose, and (ii) are bound by confidentiality obligations at least as protective as this Agreement.
- **(c) Reasonable care:** Protect the Confidential Information using at least the same care the Receiving Party uses to protect its own confidential information of similar sensitivity — and in no event less than reasonable care.

## 5. Term and Duration of Confidentiality Obligations

> **What this section does:** Sets two time limits: how long this Agreement is active, and how long the confidentiality obligations continue after it ends. These are usually different — the Agreement itself might cover a short evaluation or engagement period, while the duty to keep information secret continues well beyond that.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Using the same number for both. If the Agreement and all obligations expire together after, say, 6 months, information disclosed on day one is completely unprotected 6 months later, regardless of how sensitive it still is. Give the confidentiality obligations a longer survival period than the active term.
>
> *How to decide the confidentiality period: 2 years is the minimum most lawyers will accept for a routine contractor/advisor relationship. 3 years is a common standard. Use a longer period for especially sensitive technical information.*

## 6. Return or Destruction

> **What this section does:** Gives the Disclosing Party the right to get its information back — or confirm it has been deleted — once the relationship ends. This matters most for anything the Receiving Party downloaded, printed, or copied.

On written request from the Disclosing Party — or upon expiration or termination of this Agreement — the Receiving Party will promptly return or permanently destroy all Confidential Information in its possession (including copies and notes), and will certify in writing that it has done so if requested.

Notwithstanding the above, the Receiving Party may retain one archival copy solely for legal compliance purposes, subject to the confidentiality obligations in this Agreement.

## 7. No License, No Obligation to Deal

> **What this section does:** Two protections in one section. (a) Confirms that sharing information doesn't transfer any ownership or usage rights — the Disclosing Party keeps all its rights throughout. (b) Makes clear that neither party is committing to hire, engage, or work with the other just because this NDA was signed.

- **(a) No license:** Nothing in this Agreement grants the Receiving Party any ownership interest in, license to, or other rights in the Disclosing Party's Confidential Information or intellectual property.
- **(b) No commitment:** Neither party is obligated to proceed with any engagement, relationship, or further discussions. Either party may walk away at any time without liability.
