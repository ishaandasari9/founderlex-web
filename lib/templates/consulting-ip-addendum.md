## 3. Consultant's Retained Pre-existing IP

> **What this section does:** The affirmative statement that the Consultant keeps its pre-existing IP, full stop. This is the core protective clause for the Consultant. It prevents the Client from later arguing that anything touched during the engagement was transferred.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Relying on the Client's vendor contract for IP terms. Corporate procurement agreements often include a broad "all work product is work made for hire" clause that could sweep in your entire methodology. If a Client sends you their standard vendor agreement, read the IP clause word by word. Add the Consultant IP carve-out in writing before signing anything.
>
> *Legal basis: Under 17 U.S.C. § 101 of the US Copyright Act, independent contractors' work only qualifies as "work made for hire" in nine narrowly defined categories. Most consulting deliverables don't fall into these categories — which means absent an explicit written assignment, copyright stays with the Consultant. This Addendum resolves the ambiguity by spelling out exactly what stays with each party.*

The Consultant retains all right, title, and interest in and to all Consultant IP. Nothing in this Addendum, the referenced SOW, or any other agreement between the parties:

- Transfers ownership of any Consultant IP to the Client
- Creates a license to Consultant IP beyond the narrow license set out in Section 5 of this Addendum
- Prevents the Consultant from using the same Consultant IP in engagements with other clients

The fact that Consultant IP is incorporated into or used to structure Work Product does not transfer ownership of that Consultant IP to the Client.

## 4. Work Product Assignment

> **What this section does:** Transfers ownership of the Work Product — the deliverables the Client paid for — to the Client, upon the condition of full payment. This is what the Client is actually buying. Without this clause, copyright in the deliverables would default to the Consultant (who created them) absent a valid work-for-hire arrangement or explicit assignment.
>
> **The "upon full payment" condition — why it matters:** Ownership of the Work Product does not transfer until the Client has paid all invoices under the SOW. This is the Consultant's primary protection in a payment dispute: if the Client refuses to pay the final invoice, the Consultant can legitimately demand the Client stop using the Work Product until payment is made. Don't agree to remove this condition without understanding what you're giving up.
>
> *How to fill this in: The template language below handles this automatically. The only decision is whether to change "full payment of all fees" to "full payment of the applicable SOW milestone payment" — useful if the SOW has multiple deliverables, each with its own milestone payment, and you want ownership to transfer deliverable by deliverable rather than all at once at the end.*

**4a. Assignment**

The Consultant hereby assigns to the Client all right, title, and interest in the Work Product, including all copyright and other intellectual property rights therein, effective upon the Consultant's receipt of full payment of all fees due under the referenced SOW.

**4b. Pre-payment status**

Prior to receipt of full payment, all Work Product remains the exclusive property of the Consultant. The Client has no right to use, copy, distribute, modify, or act upon any Work Product until full payment has been received, except as set forth in a separate written agreement.

**4c. Cooperation**

Upon request and at no additional cost, the Consultant will execute such additional documents as may be reasonably necessary to evidence or perfect the Client's ownership of the Work Product following full payment.

## 5. License to Embedded Consultant IP

> **What this section does:** Grants the Client a narrow license to use the Consultant's pre-existing IP that is embedded in the Work Product — so the Client can actually use what they paid for, without getting full ownership of the Consultant's underlying methodology.
>
> **The architecture here:** The Client owns the Work Product (Section 4). But the Work Product may contain pieces of Consultant IP — for example, a report structured using the Consultant's proprietary framework. The Client needs a license to use those embedded pieces *as part of using the report*, but doesn't need (and can't have) the Consultant IP itself. This license is the bridge.
>
> **Common mistake to avoid:** Drafting the license too broadly. "License to use Consultant IP" without limiting it to "solely as embedded in the Work Product" could be read to give the Client a license to use your frameworks independently. The limiting language below prevents this — keep it in.

The Consultant grants to the Client a non-exclusive, non-transferable, perpetual license to use Embedded Consultant IP solely:

- As it exists within and forms part of the Work Product
- For the Client's internal business purposes
- In connection with the same subject matter as the engagement described in the referenced SOW

This license does **not** permit the Client to:
- Extract, isolate, or use Embedded Consultant IP independently of the Work Product
- Reproduce or distribute Consultant IP as a standalone methodology, framework, or template
- Grant sublicenses to the Embedded Consultant IP to third parties
- Adapt or modify the Embedded Consultant IP for use in new or separate projects without the Consultant's written consent
